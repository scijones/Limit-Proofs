# Formalization fidelity audit

This audit treats Lean declarations—not comments—as the specification, and compares the external interfaces with the cited results.

## Corrections made

### MCFG productions are now linear

`MCFGProduction` previously required every left-hand-side variable to occur among the right-hand-side component variables, but allowed:

* the same component variable to be assigned to several right-hand-side components; and
* a component variable to occur repeatedly in the output strings.

That formalism permits copying and is not the standard MCFG/LCFRS formalism.  The definition now requires both the flattened right-hand-side variable list and the output occurrences to be `Nodup`.  Erasure and permutation remain permitted.  This matches the copyless production condition in Seki et al. and in Aiswarya–Baumann–Saivasan–Schütze–Zetzsche.

### The headline uniform theorem now states the Grohe consequence

`main_theorem_uniform_family` previously called the Grohe interface but discarded its conclusion.  Its statement could therefore be proved without showing that any class member had a width-`k` decomposition: its grammar function was only required on decompositions supplied by a caller.

The theorem now also concludes, for the same `k`, that every class member has treewidth at most `k`.  Thus the width bound is present in the formal result rather than only in its proof narrative.

## Grohe interface

The shape of `thm_grohe` is a valid strong-hypothesis corollary of Grohe's structural CSP classification:

* Grohe's result concerns `HOM(C, -)` / structural CSP restrictions and concludes bounded treewidth modulo homomorphic equivalence (equivalently, bounded treewidth of cores), under the parameterized-complexity assumption.
* This project assumes every member is already a core (`AllCores`), so concluding actual bounded treewidth is legitimate.
* Bounded arity and recursive enumerability are represented explicitly.

However, three central notions are still opaque propositions:

* `SizedHypergraphClass.RecursivelyEnumerable`;
* `SizedHypergraphClass.UniformPolyTimeSolvable`;
* `SizedHypergraph.IsCore`.

Consequently Lean checks only the implication represented by the `thm_grohe` axiom.  It does not check that these predicates encode Turing-machine enumerability, polynomial-time solvability, relational-structure homomorphisms, or cores.  A full formalization of Grohe's theorem would need definitions of finite relational structures, homomorphisms/cores, encodings, algorithms, running time, and parameterized reductions, followed by a proof of the theorem.  Those items are not present here.

The move from a hypergraph-only object to Grohe's relational structures also suppresses relation symbols, tuple order, and repeated tuple coordinates.  This can be acceptable only as a deliberately stated abstraction boundary; it is not itself a formalization of the published theorem.

## Engelfriet / bounded-treewidth-to-MCFL interface

The current `engelfriet_tw_to_mcfl` is **not a formalization of a theorem stated in Engelfriet's work**.  It is a project-specific external assumption asserting that every rooted tree decomposition has an MCFG whose language is exactly a separately axiomatized predicate `IsTreeCompatibleOrdering`, with additional node, boundary, bag, and child fields.

The problem is structural, not merely missing proof syntax:

1. `IsTreeCompatibleOrdering` has no definition.  Its only laws say that its words are nodup vertex enumerations and that at least one exists.  No traversal, home-bag assignment, yield graph, tree automaton, HRG derivation, or MSO interpretation is encoded.
2. The cited HRG/MCFG equivalence concerns string languages generated by context-free hypergraph grammars.  It does not by itself supply the bespoke exact language of orderings attached to each arbitrary rooted decomposition.
3. The recent bounded-treewidth/MCFL result cited in the manuscript is about **MSO-definable bounded special-treewidth systems**.  Its forward construction starts from a width-`k` tree automaton and produces a `k`-MCFG (with a bounded rule rank).  Ordinary treewidth is explicitly distinguished from special treewidth.  The project has neither special tree decompositions nor tree automata nor an MSO-definability hypothesis.
4. The Lean tree-decomposition width is `max |bag| - 1`, while the cited recent paper defines its special-treewidth width as maximum bag size.  Any imported dimension bound must account for this convention shift explicitly.

Therefore all bridge and main theorems are kernel-checked consequences of the declared external assumptions, but the central Engelfriet assumption currently contains almost the entire disputed bridge.  The formalization does not establish that the assumption follows from either cited work.

A faithful replacement should choose one of two routes:

* **HRG route:** formalize sourced hypergraphs, hyperedge replacement, derivations, string yields, and the exact HRG-to-MCFG theorem; then construct an HRG whose yields really are the desired behavior orderings and prove soundness/completeness.
* **Special-treewidth/MSO route:** formalize special tree decompositions, linearly ordered labeled execution graphs, MSO interpretations/tree automata, and the tree-automaton-to-MCFG translation; then prove the agents' execution graphs satisfy the required MSO and special-treewidth hypotheses.

Neither route can be replaced faithfully by comments identifying an unconstrained predicate with “structured yields.”

## Other external assumptions and finite stitching

The project exposes the standard finite-union closure of a fixed MCFG tier as `mcfg_finite_union`.  This is used in its intended existential sense: grammars obtained from a common width-bounded ordering grammar are stitched into a grammar for their union.  No chosen satisfying assignment, derivation, or particular grammar representation must survive that step.

Accordingly, `behavior_grammar_exists` first obtains the `(k+1)`-dimensional ordering grammar, takes one homomorphic image for each satisfying assignment, and then unions those finitely many image languages.  The active `full_behavior_bound` and `main_theorem_finite_subclass` similarly union languages that have already been proved to inhabit the common `(k+1)` tier.

This use of finiteness does not determine `k`: in all headline results, `k` is the uniform treewidth bound supplied by the Grohe interface before language stitching occurs.  The statements are existence claims, not algorithms for constructing grammars or preserving solutions.  The stronger `finite_language_is_mcfl` shortcut remains absent.

The disputed Engelfriet interface remains visible: the formalization checks the deductions from that external assumption, but does not claim to derive it from the cited literature.

## Scope of what Lean currently verifies

There are no `sorry` or `admit` terms in the Lean source.  The project verifies the internal deductions from its interfaces, including locality, boundary lemmas, semantic invariance under homomorphic equivalence, the Grohe-to-treewidth corollary, and non-emptiness examples.  It does **not** verify the cited Grohe or Engelfriet mathematics because those results and several associated notions remain axiomatized or opaque.

In particular, successful compilation must not be interpreted as bibliographic fidelity: Lean proves exactly the declarations supplied to it, including external assumptions.
